## How this calorie calculator works

First, we estimate how many calories your body burns at rest. This is your BMR. We use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which looks at your sex, age, height, and weight. A 2005 review found it was the most likely of the common formulas to land within 10% of measured values in healthy adults.

Next, we multiply your BMR by your activity level. The factors range from 1.2 if you sit most of the day to 1.9 if you train hard every day or have a physical job. That gives your maintenance calories. Eat about this much and your weight should stay steady.

## A calorie calculator to lose weight or gain

Below your maintenance number, you'll see a table of daily targets:

- **Lose fat (our default):** about 20% below maintenance.
- **Lose 1 lb (0.45 kg) a week:** about 500 calories below maintenance.
- **Lose 0.5 lb (0.23 kg) a week:** about 250 calories below maintenance.
- **Maintain:** your maintenance number.
- **Gain 0.5 or 1 lb (0.23 or 0.45 kg) a week:** about 250 or 500 calories above maintenance.

The weekly targets use the rough rule that 1 lb of body weight equals about 3,500 calories (7,700 per kg). We never go below 1,200 calories for women or 1,500 for men. If a target hits that floor, it's marked with a star.

## What your result means

Your number is a good first guess, not a fact. Even the best formula can be off by 10% or more for some people. Weight loss also slows over time as your body adapts, so the weekly rates are best seen as a guide.

Pick a target, stick with it for 2 to 3 weeks, and weigh yourself a few times a week at the same time of day. If the scale isn't moving the way you expect, change your calories by 100 to 200 a day.
