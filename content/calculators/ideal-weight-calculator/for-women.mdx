## The female formulas

This page starts with the female setting, so you see the women's versions of four classic formulas. Each one starts with a weight for a woman who is 5 feet (152 cm) tall and adds a set amount for each extra inch (2.54 cm):

- **Devine (1974):** 45.5 kg plus 2.3 kg per inch
- **Robinson (1983):** 49 kg plus 1.7 kg per inch
- **Miller (1983):** 53.1 kg plus 1.36 kg per inch
- **Hamwi (1964):** 45.5 kg plus 2.2 kg per inch

For a woman who is 5 ft 5 in (165 cm), they range from about 125 to 132 lb (56.5 to 59.9 kg). The healthy BMI range for the same height is much wider: about 111 to 150 lb (50.4 to 67.9 kg).

These formulas were built to help doctors dose medicines. They are not a target you have to hit. They also aren't meant for women under 5 feet tall.

## Ideal weight by age

Many women search for an ideal weight by age. The honest answer is that the formulas and the adult BMI cut-offs don't change with age. The CDC applies the same BMI categories to all adults 20 and older.

Age still matters for your health, though. A 2014 review of adults 65 and older found that a BMI in the overweight range was not linked to a higher risk of death, while a BMI under 23 was. For older women, keeping muscle and strength often matters more than reaching a lower number.

- Use the healthy range as a broad guide, not a single goal.
