## How this keto calculator works

This keto calculator works in grams, not fixed percentages. It takes three steps.

**1. Calories.** We estimate your maintenance calories with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and your activity level. Then we adjust for your goal. Lose fat takes off 20%, and build muscle adds 10%. We never go below 1,200 calories a day for women or 1,500 for men.

**2. Carbs and protein.** You pick a carb limit of 20, 30, or 50 grams. Protein comes from our [protein calculator](/protein-calculator). We use the middle of the range for your goal. If your BMI is over 30, we base protein on the weight you would be at a BMI of 30, since body fat does not need extra protein.

**3. Fat.** Fat fills the rest of your calories. Carbs and protein have 4 calories per gram, and fat has 9. So we subtract the carb and protein calories from your total and divide what is left by 9.

## What your result means

Your result shows grams and the share of calories for each macro. Hit your carb limit and protein target first. Fat is the flexible part. On a fat loss plan, you do not need to force in extra fat if you feel full.

Your fat share may come out lower than the 70% to 80% you see on some keto charts. That is because we give protein more room to help protect muscle. Your carbs will still be low enough to count as keto.

## Choosing your carb limit

- **20 g:** the strictest option. Mostly meat, fish, eggs, cheese, and leafy greens.
- **30 g:** a bit more room for vegetables, nuts, and berries.
- **50 g:** the upper edge of keto, with the most room for vegetables and fruit.

Count total carbs, and read labels on sauces, drinks, and "low carb" snacks.

## Tips for keto

- Build meals around protein, then add low-carb vegetables.
- Plan a few go-to meals so you are not guessing when you are hungry.
- Eating out? Pick a protein, skip the bread or bun, and swap fries for a salad or vegetables.
