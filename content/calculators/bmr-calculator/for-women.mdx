## Why the formulas treat women differently

BMR formulas were built by measuring how many calories real men and women burn at rest. Women tend to carry a higher share of body fat. The American Council on Exercise lists 25% to 31% body fat as average for women and 18% to 24% for men. Lean mass, which includes muscle and organs, drives most of the calories you burn at rest.

That helps explain why the female formulas give a lower number at the same size. With Mifflin-St Jeor, a woman gets 166 fewer calories a day than a man of the same height, weight, and age.

## What this version shows you

This page starts with the female setting. For a 40-year-old woman who is 5 ft 5 in (165 cm) and 145 lb (66 kg), you will see:

- **Mifflin-St Jeor:** about 1,329 calories a day. A 2005 review found this was the most likely of the common formulas to land within 10% of measured values.
- **Harris-Benedict (revised):** about 1,394 calories a day.
- **Katch-McArdle:** shows up once you add your body fat %.

Here the first two results are about 65 calories apart. Formulas rarely match exactly, which is a good reminder that every BMR is an estimate. We highlight Mifflin-St Jeor because of that review.
