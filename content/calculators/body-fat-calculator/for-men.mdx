## Why this version is simpler

The men's Navy formula needs just three numbers: your height, neck, and waist. The women's version also needs hips. That makes this one of the quickest body fat checks you can do at home with a tape measure.

This page starts with the male setting. With the sample numbers (5 ft 10 in or 178 cm tall, a 15 in or 38 cm neck, and a 34 in or 86 cm waist), the estimate is about 17.5%. That is just under the ACE average range of 18% to 24%, which the calculator calls "below average (lean)."

## Reading your result

The ACE ranges for men who are not athletes:

- **Below about 6%:** may be dangerously low
- **18% to 24%:** average
- **25% or more:** obesity range

You'll also see your fat mass and your lean mass (everything else, including muscle, bone, and water) in pounds or kilograms.

## How to measure

- **Waist:** around the belly button, relaxed, after a normal breath out. Don't suck in.
- **Neck:** just below the voice box, with the tape sloping slightly down to the front.

Measure each spot 2 or 3 times and use the average. Use the same tape and time of day when you check again.

Your waist size on its own is useful too. The WHO links a waist over 94 cm (37 in) in men with increased health risk, and over 102 cm (40 in) with substantially increased risk.
