## Why women's ranges are higher

Women naturally carry more body fat than men. That is why the "average" range for women sits well above the range for men: 25% to 31% for women versus 18% to 24% for men, according to ACE.

So don't compare your number with a man's, or with a fitness model's photo. Compare it with the ranges for women, and with your own past results.

## What this version sets up for you

This page starts with the female setting, which uses the women's Navy formula. It needs four numbers: height, neck, waist, and hips. The men's version skips the hips.

With the sample numbers (5 ft 5 in or 165 cm tall, a 13 in or 33 cm neck, a 30 in or 76 cm waist, and 38 in or 97 cm hips), the estimate is about 28.6%. That sits in the ACE average range. You'll also see how much of your weight is fat and how much is lean mass.

## How to measure

- **Waist:** at the narrowest point, relaxed, after a normal breath out.
- **Hips:** at the widest point around your buttocks.
- **Neck:** just below the voice box, with the tape sloping slightly down to the front.

Measure each spot 2 or 3 times and use the average. Skip this during pregnancy, since your waist and hips change for other reasons.

Your waist alone also tells you something. The WHO links a waist over 80 cm (31.5 in) in women with increased health risk. Our [waist-to-hip ratio calculator](/waist-to-hip-ratio-calculator) looks at this in more detail.
