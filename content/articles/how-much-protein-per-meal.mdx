### How much protein per meal should I eat?

Protein per meal is your daily protein range divided by the number of meals you eat. A 180 lb (82 kg) person building muscle has a daily range of 130 to 180 g. That works out to about 43 to 60 g per meal over 3 meals, or about 33 to 45 g over 4 meals.

Picture a common day. Coffee and toast for breakfast, a light sandwich at lunch, then a big chicken dinner that carries almost all the protein. The daily total might look fine. But you don't need a complicated plan to spread it out, just your daily number and a little division.

## How do you work out protein per meal?

Start with a daily protein range based on your body weight and goal. Then divide it by your meals. Here at Protein Per Meal, these are the ranges our [protein calculator](/protein-calculator) uses, in grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day:

| Goal | Daily range | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Maintain weight | 0.8 to 1.2 g/kg | 0.8 is the adult RDA (Institute of Medicine) |
| Build muscle | 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg | Morton 2018 meta-analysis |
| Lose fat | 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg | Morton 2018 and the ISSN position stand, to help keep muscle |
| On a GLP-1 medicine | 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg | 2025 joint advisory from four US groups |
| Healthy aging (50+) | 1.0 to 1.2 g/kg | PROT-AGE Study Group |

One detail is worth knowing. If your BMI is over 30, the calculator uses the weight you would be at a BMI of 30 for your height. The 2025 GLP-1 advisory notes that full body weight can overestimate protein needs in people with obesity.

Then the calculator does the simple part. It rounds your daily range to the nearest 5 g. It splits that across **3 or 4 meals**, whichever you choose.

## How much protein per meal for gym-goers, adults 50+, and GLP-1 users?

It ranges from about 18 g to 60 g per meal across these three sample people. The numbers come from our protein calculator. They are illustrations, not personal advice.

## What does research say about the best protein per meal?

Research points to roughly 0.24 to 0.55 g per kg per meal, depending on your age and goal. Dividing by meals is practical. But is there a "best" amount per sitting? This is where it gets interesting.

### Do older adults need more protein per meal?

Yes, the research suggests they do. A 2015 analysis by Daniel Moore and colleagues in Canada, the UK, and the US looked back at their lab data on muscle protein building after a single protein dose. The response leveled off at about **0.24 g per kg of body weight in younger men**. In older men (around age 71), it leveled off at about **0.40 g/kg**.

For a 150 lb (68 kg) person, that's about 16 g per meal when young. It's about 27 g per meal when older. The authors concluded that older men are less sensitive to low protein intakes. The study only included men, so treat it as a rough guide for women.

Look back at the 66-year-old example. Her 3-meal split of 23 to 27 g lines up well with that older-adult number. Her 4-meal split of 18 to 20 g falls a bit short.

My take: if you're older and find it hard to eat a lot at once, 3 solid protein meals may be easier than 4 small ones. Our guide on [protein after 50](/guides/protein-after-50) goes deeper.

### How much protein per meal to build muscle?

About 0.4 g/kg per meal, across at least 4 meals. That comes from a 2018 review by Brad Schoenfeld and Alan Aragon on how much protein the body can use in one meal for building muscle. The goal is to reach at least 1.6 g/kg a day. At the upper end of 2.2 g/kg a day, that becomes about **0.55 g/kg per meal**.

For our 180 lb (82 kg) lifter, that's about 33 to 45 g per meal. That matches the calculator's 4-meal split. Nice when the math agrees.

### Can your body only use 25 g of protein at once?

No, that isn't a hard limit. You may have heard that your body can only use about 20 to 25 g of protein at once. Schoenfeld and Aragon note that this idea came from studies of fast-digesting protein eaten on its own. Newer research doesn't back it up as a hard limit.

A 2023 study by Jorn Trommelen and colleagues at Maastricht University compared 100 g and 25 g of protein after exercise. The 100 g dose led to a **bigger and longer muscle-building response, lasting more than 12 hours**. The researchers also reported that the larger dose had a negligible effect on how much protein was burned for energy.

So a big protein dinner isn't wasted. Still, spreading protein out makes the daily total easier to reach for most people.

### What do experts still not know?

The best timing of protein is still unsettled. The PROT-AGE Study Group said in 2013 that the evidence on protein timing was not yet strong enough for specific recommendations. So treat per-meal targets as a helpful tool, not a strict rule. Your daily total is still the number the guidelines are built on.

## What do 25, 35, and 45 g of protein look like?

About 4 oz (113 g) of roast chicken breast gives you 35 g. The table shows protein amounts for other common foods, based on USDA FoodData Central.

| Food | Portion | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Roast chicken breast | 4 oz (113 g) | 35 g |
| Baked salmon | 5 oz (142 g) | 31 g |
| Cottage cheese, 2% | 1 cup (226 g) | 24 g |
| Plain nonfat Greek yogurt | 1 cup (226 g) | 23 g |
| Canned tuna in water, drained | 4 oz (113 g) | 22 g |
| Firm tofu | 4 oz (113 g) | 20 g |
| Eggs | 3 large (150 g) | 19 g |
| Lentils, cooked | 1 cup (198 g) | 18 g |
| Paneer | 3.5 oz (100 g) | 16 g |
| 2% milk | 1 cup (8 fl oz, 237 mL) | 8 g |

A few easy combos:

- **About 27 g:** 3 eggs plus 2 slices of whole-wheat bread (64 g, about 8 g protein).
- **About 35 g:** 4 oz (113 g) chicken breast in a wrap or salad.
- **About 45 g:** 1 cup (226 g) Greek yogurt plus 4 oz (113 g) canned tuna across a meal. Or 5 oz (142 g) salmon plus 1 cup of lentils (49 g).

## How can GLP-1 users get enough protein with a small appetite?

Eat the protein part of each meal first. That simple tip comes from the 2025 joint advisory. On a GLP-1 medicine, big meals may just not fit, so starting with **protein-rich foods** makes it more likely you get enough before you feel full.

The advisory also encourages protein from beans, peas, lentils, whole grains, dairy, seafood, eggs, and lean poultry. Choosing 4 smaller meals over 3 may help here too. Our guide on [protein on Ozempic](/guides/protein-on-ozempic) has more food-first ideas.

<Callout title="Check with your care team">
If you have kidney disease, diabetes, or are pregnant, or you take any medicine, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian about your protein target. PROT-AGE notes that people with severe kidney disease who are not on dialysis may need to limit protein.
</Callout>

## So how much protein per meal should you aim for?

Take your daily range and divide it by the meals you actually eat. In the three examples, that ranged from about 18 to 60 g per meal. Older adults may do better with a bit more at each sitting. Gym-goers can aim for about 0.4 g/kg across 4 meals.

Don't stress about hitting a perfect number every time. Build each meal around a protein food and keep your daily total in range. Get your personal per-meal numbers in our [protein calculator](/protein-calculator).
