## Why protein matters more as you age

As we get older, our bodies get less efficient at using protein to build and repair muscle. Illness can raise protein needs even more.

Keeping muscle helps you stay strong, keep your balance, and do everyday things on your own. Eating enough protein, along with staying active, is one of the best ways to protect it.

## How much protein do you need after 50?

The basic minimum for all adults (the RDA) is 0.8 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight a day. But an international group of experts on aging, called PROT-AGE, looked at the research for older adults. Their advice for people over 65:

- **Most older adults:** at least **1.0 to 1.2 g per kg** a day.
- **Active older adults:** **1.2 g per kg** or more.
- **Older adults with a short- or long-term illness:** often **1.2 to 1.5 g per kg**.
- **Severe kidney disease (not on dialysis):** may need to **limit** protein. Follow your doctor’s advice.

Our [protein calculator](/protein-calculator?goal=aging) uses at least 1.0 g per kg for anyone 50 or older, as a simple starting point.

### What that looks like

| Body weight | 1.0 g/kg | 1.2 g/kg |
|---|---|---|
| 130 lb (59 kg) | 59 g | 71 g |
| 150 lb (68 kg) | 68 g | 82 g |
| 180 lb (82 kg) | 82 g | 98 g |
| 200 lb (91 kg) | 91 g | 109 g |

## Spread it across your day

Many people eat most of their protein at dinner. Spreading it out makes the daily total easier to reach. Try to include a protein food at breakfast, lunch, and dinner.

## Easy protein foods

These foods are soft, easy to make, and high in protein:

| Food | Portion | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Cottage cheese | 1 cup (226 g) | 24 g |
| Plain Greek yogurt, nonfat | 1 cup (226 g) | 23 g |
| Canned tuna in water, drained | 4 oz (113 g) | 22 g |
| Firm tofu | 4 oz (113 g) | 20 g |
| Eggs | 3 large | 19 g |
| Lentils, cooked | 1 cup (198 g) | 18 g |
| Milk (2% / semi-skimmed) | 1 cup (244 g) | 8 g |

Values come from USDA FoodData Central.

## Stay active

PROT-AGE recommends both endurance exercise (like walking or cycling) and strength exercise (like light weights or resistance bands) at a level that is safe for you. Protein and exercise work best together. Ask your doctor what is right for you.

## The bottom line

After 50, aim for at least 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight a day, spread across your meals, and pair it with regular exercise. If you have kidney disease or other health conditions, talk to your doctor first.
