Ask three people how many carbs they eat on keto and you might hear three answers: "basically zero," "under 50," and "only net carbs count." Then a snack bar label says "2 g net carbs" next to a much bigger total carb number. No wonder people get confused about how many carbs on keto is the right amount.

Here's the good news. The range is well described, and once you understand a few terms, it gets simple. Let me walk you through it.

## What keto actually is

A ketogenic (keto) diet is a very low-carb, high-fat way of eating. Harvard explains that when stored glucose runs low, the liver makes ketone bodies from fat, which the body can use for fuel. When ketones build up in the blood, that is called ketosis.

Harvard's Nutrition Source points out that there is no single "standard" keto diet. Different plans set slightly different targets. But they all cut carbs hard, and they all land in a similar zone.

## How many carbs on keto? The usual range

According to Harvard's Nutrition Source, a ketogenic diet:

- **Typically keeps total carbs under 50 grams a day.**
- **Can go as low as 20 grams a day.**

Popular keto resources suggest this split of daily calories:

| Macro | Share of calories |
|---|---|
| Fat | 70% to 80% |
| Protein | 10% to 20% |
| Carbohydrate | 5% to 10% |

Harvard gives an example. On a 2,000-calorie diet, that works out to about 165 g of fat, 40 g of carbs, and 75 g of protein.

To put 50 g in context, the Institute of Medicine sets the RDA for carbs at 130 g a day for adults, with a normal range of 45% to 65% of calories. Keto sits far below that on purpose. Our guide on [how many carbs per day](/guides/how-many-carbs-per-day) covers the standard numbers.

## Finding your own keto carb number

Because the percentages depend on your calories, your carb limit shifts with your size and activity. Here's how to work it out.

1. Find your daily calories with our [TDEE calculator](/tdee-calculator).
2. Multiply by 0.05 and by 0.10.
3. Divide each number by 4 (each gram of carb has 4 calories).

I ran two sample people through the Mifflin-St Jeor equation our calculators use, with the site's 20% weight-loss adjustment. These are examples only.

### A woman, 170 lb (77 kg), 5 ft 4 in (163 cm), age 40, lightly active

- Weight-loss calories: about 1,570 a day.
- 5% to 10% of 1,570 = **about 20 to 39 g of carbs a day**.

### A man, 210 lb (95 kg), 5 ft 10 in (178 cm), age 40, lightly active

- Weight-loss calories: about 2,060 a day.
- 5% to 10% of 2,060 = **about 26 to 52 g of carbs a day**.

This is where it gets interesting. The man's top number edges past 50 g. That's why Harvard's simple rule makes a handy check: stay under 50 g, and go lower if your plan calls for it. Our [keto calculator](/keto-calculator) lets you pick a daily limit of 20, 30, or 50 g, then builds your protein and fat around it.

## Net carbs vs total carbs

This part trips up a lot of people, so let me explain it plainly.

- **Total carbs** is the full carbohydrate number on a food label.
- **Net carbs** is total carbs minus fiber (and sometimes minus sugar alcohols).

Fiber is a carb your body can't fully digest, so some keto plans don't count it. That's the idea behind net carbs.

Here's the catch. Harvard notes that "net carbs" and "impact carbs" are unregulated terms created by food companies. The math is not exact, because some sugar alcohols can still add calories and raise blood sugar. Even within the keto community, people debate whether to use net carbs at all.

My take, based on what I've seen in the research: if you are new to keto, counting total carbs is the simpler and stricter option. If you count net carbs, do it with whole foods like vegetables and nuts, not with "low net carb" snack bars.

## What 20 to 40 g of carbs looks like in real food

Numbers on a screen feel abstract. So here is a sample keto day built from USDA FoodData Central values. It is one example, not a meal plan.

| Meal | Food | Total carbs | Fiber |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 2 large eggs (100 g) | 0.7 g | 0 g |
| Breakfast | 1 cup raw spinach (30 g) | 1.1 g | 0.7 g |
| Breakfast | 1 oz cheddar (28 g) | 0.9 g | 0 g |
| Lunch | 4 oz roast chicken breast (113 g) | 0 g | 0 g |
| Lunch | 1 cup cooked broccoli (156 g) | 11.2 g | 5.1 g |
| Lunch | 3.5 oz avocado (100 g) | 8.5 g | 6.7 g |
| Snack | 1 oz almonds (28 g) | 6.1 g | 3.5 g |
| Dinner | 5 oz baked salmon (142 g) | 0 g | 0 g |
| Dinner | 1 cup raw cauliflower (107 g) | 5.3 g | 2.1 g |
| Dessert | ½ cup strawberries (76 g) | 5.8 g | 1.5 g |
| **Total** | | **about 40 g** | **about 20 g** |

That's about 40 g of total carbs, or about 20 g of net carbs. Same food, two very different numbers. This is exactly why it helps to know which one your plan uses.

It also shows something encouraging. A keto day with lots of vegetables, avocado, and nuts can still reach about 20 g of fiber. That is short of the 25 g adequate intake for women 19 to 50 and the 38 g for men, but it's far better than a keto day built only on meat and cheese. Our [fiber calculator](/fiber-calculator) shows your own target.

### Where the carbs hide

For contrast, here are a few foods from the same USDA data:

- 1 cup (158 g) cooked white rice: 45 g carbs
- 1 medium (118 g) banana: 27 g carbs
- 1 slice (32 g) whole-wheat bread: 14 g carbs

One cup of rice alone would use up most of a 50 g day. Harvard lists grains, starchy vegetables, fruit juice, most fruits, beans, and lentils as foods that keto plans limit or skip.

## What about protein on keto?

Keto keeps protein moderate, usually 10% to 20% of calories. Harvard explains why: your body can turn some protein into glucose, so very high protein may make ketosis harder.

That creates a real trade-off if you also want to keep muscle while losing weight. Our keto calculator takes its protein number from our [protein calculator](/protein-calculator), which can land above 20% of calories. That choice puts keeping muscle first. Neither approach is "wrong." They just serve different goals, and a registered dietitian can help you balance them.

## Eating out on keto

Many chains now list protein-heavy items with very few carbs. From the chain data on our site:

- **Chipotle:** High Protein Cup, Chicken (4 oz): 0 g carbs, 32 g protein, 180 calories.
- **Chick-fil-A:** 8 ct Grilled Nuggets: 1 g carbs, 25 g protein, 130 calories. The Hash Brown Scramble Bowl with Grilled Filet, no hash browns: 2 g carbs, 29 g protein.
- **Subway:** Grilled Chicken Protein Bowl: 12 g carbs, 48 g protein, 620 calories.
- **Starbucks:** Bacon & Gruyère Egg Bites: 9 g carbs, 19 g protein.

Watch the sauces and sides. They are where carbs sneak back in.

## Things to know before you start

Harvard lists some common early symptoms of cutting carbs this low. They include hunger, tiredness, low mood, irritability, constipation, headaches, and "brain fog." These can last days to weeks, and they may ease with time.

It also flags possible long-term concerns, such as kidney stones, bone loss, higher uric acid (linked to gout), and gaps in fiber, B vitamins, iron, magnesium, and zinc. Eating a wide mix of allowed foods helps.

<Callout title="Talk to your doctor first if this applies to you">
If you have type 1 or type 2 diabetes, kidney or liver disease, a history of an eating disorder, or you are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before trying keto. This also matters if you take any medicines, especially ones that lower blood sugar.
</Callout>

## The bottom line on how many carbs on keto

So, how many carbs on keto? Most plans keep you under 50 g of total carbs a day, and stricter ones go down to about 20 g. In percentage terms, that's roughly 5% to 10% of your calories.

Pick a plan, decide whether you're counting total or net carbs, and build your day around protein, low-starch vegetables, and healthy fats. Try our [keto calculator](/keto-calculator) to get your own starting number. You've got this.
